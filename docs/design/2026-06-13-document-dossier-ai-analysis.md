## Problem Statement

A bidder who has found a property on AuctionScope still cannot answer the one
question that decides whether they bid: "is this property's paperwork clean, and
what am I missing before the auction closes?" Today the platform ingests exactly
one bank-published artifact per listing (the sale notice). It does not help a
user assemble and reason over the full document dossier — bank docs, title chain,
revenue records (Patta / Chitta / FMB), Encumbrance Certificate, approvals, tax,
legal opinions — that real diligence requires. The buyer is left to collect and
vet ~50 document types across 9 categories from scattered sources (the bank, the
sub-registrar, TNREGINET, TN eServices, the apartment association) with no
synthesis and no safety net.

The feature: a private, per-property **document locker** where a user uploads
whatever they have collected, the system OCRs and auto-classifies each document
into the 9 diligence categories, shows a **have/missing checklist** against the
"absolute minimum" set, surfaces a **Diligence Readiness Score**, deep-links the
user to the right government portal to fetch what is missing, and lets them ask
questions grounded in their own documents. Formal AI verdicts (legal opinion,
valuation, full due diligence) are a deliberate Phase-2 build on top of this
substrate.

## Demand Evidence

- **Pain is observed, not hypothetical.** The user has watched bidders (and
  themselves) do this work: "I've seen the pain" — manually collecting documents,
  paying advocates for legal opinions, struggling, with the pain real and
  expensive but not yet voiced as a feature request.
- **The workaround splits by stakes** (status quo, Q2): big-ticket buyers hire a
  property advocate (~₹5k–25k, days-to-weeks turnaround, often too slow for a
  ~2-week auction window); smaller/faster buyers DIY across scattered govt
  portals; many simply **wing it / skip diligence** and eat the risk of title
  defects, encumbrances, and litigation.
- The deepest signal is domain depth, not a single quote: the user supplied a
  precise 9-category, ~50-document taxonomy with an "absolute minimum I would not
  skip" subset — the kind of map you only have if you've lived this problem.

Honest gap: no specific *named* user requesting this yet, and no paying-for-it
proof. This is a "seen the pain" bet, not a "user X demanded it" bet. The
assignment below is designed to convert it into harder evidence.

## Status Quo

Per Q2, a serious bidder vetting one property does some mix of:
1. **Hire an advocate** — legal opinion + title search, ~₹5k–25k, slow.
2. **DIY across portals** — EC from TNREGINET; Patta / Chitta / FMB from TN
   eServices; tax receipts; etc. Scattered, manual, no synthesis.
3. **Wing it / skip it** — bid with incomplete vetting because proper diligence
   is too slow or expensive.

The real competitor is option 3 (the spreadsheet-and-WhatsApp-and-gut workaround),
not another app.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** an active SARFAESI auction bidder (individual investor or small
syndicate) chasing a *specific* property with a hard auction deadline, who needs
confidence about the paperwork faster and cheaper than an advocate can deliver.

**Narrowest wedge (validated in session):** the **Document Locker + completeness
tracker** — collect, OCR, auto-classify into the 9 categories, flag missing vs
the minimum checklist, ask questions over what's there. Explicitly NOT a verdict
engine in v1. This is the substrate every later analysis (legal opinion,
valuation, due diligence) plugs into; you cannot produce any of those without a
collected, classified, OCR'd dossier first.

## Constraints

- Production product (auctionscope.in) with real users; ships inside the existing
  app, not as a separate product.
- Existing stack: FastAPI (`api/main.py` + focused routers), PydanticAI agent
  (`api/agent.py`, `api/tools/`), Neo4j Aura knowledge graph, Cloudflare R2
  (currently a *public* notices bucket), Supabase auth, OCR/MinerU + vision-LLM
  enrichment pipeline (`pipeline/ocr_extract.py`, `mineru.py`,
  `classify_notice.py`, `extract_descriptions.py`), no-build vanilla-JS SPA.
- User documents are confidential (title deeds, EC, legal heir certs) and must be
  access-controlled — private storage with signed URLs, never the public bucket,
  never merged into the shared public auction graph.
- Indian / Tamil Nadu property-law domain: SARFAESI, MODT, Patta, Chitta, FMB,
  TSLR, EC, UDS, DTCP/CMDA, RERA, CC/OC.
- Liability: v1 must not present itself as a legal or valuation authority.

## Premises

All six confirmed by the user (re-explained in plain language and agreed):

1. **Locker-first, verdicts-later.** v1 = upload → OCR → auto-classify into 9
   categories → flag missing vs the "absolute minimum" → Q&A over what's there.
   Legal opinion / valuation / formal due diligence are Phase 2 on this substrate.
2. **The "what's missing" alert is the killer feature**, more than Q&A. Telling a
   bidder what they're missing *before* they bid is what saves them money.
3. **User dossiers are private and must NOT enter the shared public auction
   graph.** Per-user, per-property, access-controlled, architecturally separate.
4. **A dossier attaches to a property that may not be in the graph.** Must work
   for an existing scraped property *and* a user-created off-platform property.
5. **Reuse, not rebuild.** New work = private upload/storage, a doc→category
   classifier, a completeness-checklist model, and a dossier Q&A tool wired into
   the existing agent. OCR pipeline and agent already exist.
6. **v1 explicitly disclaims legal/valuation conclusions.** Framing is "checklist
   + document assistant," not "advocate replacement." Verdicts come later with
   citations and disclaimers.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Document Locker MVP
New private per-property dossier. Upload → reuse OCR → LLM classifies each doc into
the 9 categories → checklist shows have/missing vs the minimum → agent answers
questions grounded in the user's docs. Effort M-L. Risk Low-Med. Reuses the OCR
pipeline, R2, agent, auth, and the conversations pattern. Forward-compatible: the
per-document records become the input for Phase-2 verdicts. Risk: classification
accuracy across ~50 doc types needs eval/tuning; a pure "sort + list gaps" locker
can feel passive.

### Approach B: Full Dossier Pipeline
A, plus per-doc-type **structured field extraction** (EC → transaction rows; sale
deed → parties / survey no / extent; Patta → owner / patta no), async processing,
a per-user vector index, and a **cross-document reconciler** that flags mismatches
(name on Patta ≠ name on sale deed). Effort L-XL. Risk Med. The reconciliation
flags are real diligence value and the structured fields are exactly what Phase-2
legal opinion / valuation need. But it's a big surface, the per-doc-type prompts +
evals are substantial, and it overshoots the validated wedge before a single real
EC has been uploaded.

### Approach C: Readiness Coach (creative/lateral)
Workflow-first. Lead with a **Diligence Readiness Score (0–100)** per property and
an active "go get this next" flow that **deep-links to the actual govt portals**
(TNREGINET for EC, TN eServices for Patta / Chitta / FMB) with survey / sub-registrar
details pre-filled from the scraped notice. OCR is light — just confirm "this IS an
EC dated X for survey Y." Effort S-M. Risk Low. Ships fastest, turns a passive
locker into an active assistant, and the portal pre-fill is a genuine moat. But
light OCR means weaker Q&A depth, and portal deep-links can break when those sites
change.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A as the shipping target, with Approach C's Readiness Score +
"go-get-it" govt-portal deep-links as the UI hero, and the data model shaped so
Approach B drops in later.**

Rationale: A ships the exact wedge the user validated. C's readiness score and
portal deep-links cost little and are what make Premise 2 (the missing-doc alert)
feel *active* instead of passive — the difference between a filing cabinet and an
assistant. B is the 2-3 month trajectory once real dossiers reveal which doc types
and which cross-document mismatches actually matter; building B now means guessing
at field schemas before any real document has been seen.

### Sketch of the build (for the eng review that follows)

- **Storage:** a NEW **private** R2 bucket/prefix, separate from the existing
  public notices bucket. This is net-new code, not reuse: `pipeline/storage.py`
  today only exposes `public_url_for()` against `R2_PUBLIC_BASE_URL` and has no
  presigned-URL path. New work = a private bucket + credentials + a
  `generate_presigned_url` read path with short TTLs. Never public.
- **Data model (Neo4j, user-scoped):** `(:User)-[:OWNS]->(:Dossier)
  -[:FOR_PROPERTY]->(:AuctionProperty | :UserProperty)`, and
  `(:Dossier)-[:CONTAINS]->(:Document {category, doc_type, r2_key, ocr_text,
  classification_confidence, status, uploaded_at})`. Note the existing graph node
  is `:AuctionProperty` (per the ontology), not `:Property`. `:UserProperty`
  covers off-graph properties (Premise 4); when the target is a `:UserProperty`
  with no scraped survey/sub-registrar data, the go-get-it deep-links fall back
  to a bare portal link (no pre-fill) and Q&A simply has less property context.
  Document nodes carry an empty-for-now `extracted_fields` map so Approach B is
  additive, not a migration.
- **Access control (explicit):** every dossier/document read is gated by an
  ownership predicate `(:User {supabase_id})-[:OWNS]->(:Dossier {id})` — the same
  `ctx.deps.supabase_id` pattern the existing `watchlist`/`watch_property` tools
  use. A request passing another user's `dossier_id` resolves to zero rows.
  Signed URLs are minted only after that ownership check and scoped to the single
  object. This is how "zero cross-user leakage" is enforced, not just asserted.
- **Ingest:** upload → validate → private R2 → OCR → classify → persist.
  - *Validation:* MIME allowlist (`pdf/jpg/jpeg/png/jfif` — the only types MinerU
    accepts), per-file size cap, per-dossier and per-user storage quota.
  - *OCR:* reuse the MinerU path (`pipeline/mineru_api.py`); server-side
    invocation is already proven (`api/review/blocks.py` calls it live for
    single-block re-extract). See the **Third-party OCR data handling** subsection
    below — this is a confidentiality decision, not a plumbing detail.
  - *Classify:* a NEW doc-type classifier (sibling module). `classify_notice.py`
    is NOT reusable here — it is a binary single-vs-multi-property notice
    classifier and has no doc-type taxonomy scaffolding. The new classifier is an
    LLM prompt seeded with the taxonomy in Appendix A.
  - *Sync vs async:* see **Ingest execution model** below — the free-tier host
    forces a decision here; the "just make it async" hand-wave does not survive.
- **Checklist + score:** encode the taxonomy (Appendix A) and the minimum set
  (Appendix B) as config; compute have/missing. **Readiness score formula:** the
  score is driven by the **10 user-uploadable minimum items** (Appendix B items
  1–9 and 12). Each present = `100/10 = 10` points, capped at 100, so a fully
  diligent v1 user can reach a true 100 ("you have 8 of 10 must-haves = 80").
  Items 10 & 11 (Advocate Legal Opinion, Court Case Search Report) are Phase-2
  *outputs* the user can't upload yet — they appear in the checklist as advisory
  "recommended, here's how to get one" rows that do NOT subtract from the score.
  Non-minimum docs present show as green "extra" but add nothing. Render
  riskiest-gap-first (missing minimum items before missing nice-to-haves).
- **Go-get-it (v1 = bare deep-links):** for each missing doc, a config-driven
  deep-link to the relevant portal (TNREGINET for EC; TN eServices for
  Patta/Chitta/FMB). **Field pre-fill is a fast-follow, NOT v1** — Open Question 4
  flags that URL-param pre-fill may not be possible on those portals, so v1 ships
  the safe version (deep-link only) and pre-fill is added once OQ4 is answered.
- **Q&A:** new agent tool `query_user_dossier(dossier_id)` that retrieves the
  owner's OCR'd docs for that dossier and answers grounded questions — reuse the
  existing `@agent.tool` + `RunContext[ChatDeps]` pattern; ownership-gated as
  above. Snippet-level citations are aspirational for v1; v1 ships grounded
  answers, citation UI is a fast-follow.
- **Frontend:** new auth-gated SPA surface — "My Dossiers" list + per-property
  dossier view (upload, readiness score, checklist, chat). No build step.
- **Disclaimer:** persistent "not legal/valuation advice" framing on the surface.

### Third-party OCR data handling (confidentiality decision)

MinerU is an external hosted API: each uploaded file is sent to MinerU's own
cloud storage before markdown is returned (`pipeline/mineru_api.py` posts to
`/file-urls/batch` then PUTs to signed OSS URLs). For confidential user
documents (title deeds, EC, legal heir certs) this is a larger exposure than the
public R2 bucket the design otherwise forbids. This must be resolved before
ingest ships, with one of:
1. A data-processing agreement + retention/deletion guarantee from MinerU, plus
   explicit user consent at upload time; OR
2. A self-hosted / on-prem OCR path for confidential categories; OR
3. Documented, user-consented accepted risk for v1, with the most sensitive
   categories (title, legal heir) excluded from third-party OCR until (1)/(2).
The doc does not pre-pick; this is an eng-review + legal decision. What is NOT
acceptable is shipping confidential docs through a third party silently.

### Ingest execution model (hosting reality)

`render.yaml` runs `plan: free`. Free Render instances spin down on idle and
restart, which kills in-process work — and FastAPI `BackgroundTasks` is the only
async mechanism present in the codebase (no Celery/RQ/Redis/arq). A multi-minute
40-page MinerU job on `BackgroundTasks` will be killed mid-flight on this plan.
Two honest options for v1:
- **(a) Synchronous-with-caps:** cap pages/size so a single ingest fits inside
  request limits; user waits. Simplest, no new infra, but caps document size.
- **(b) Paid plan + a real worker** (move to a paid Render service or add a
  queue/worker): handles large deeds, but is new infra + cost.
Pick one at eng review. The earlier "async/status-tracked" framing assumed a
worker that does not exist; the `status` field on `:Document` stays useful in
both options (queued/ocr/classified/ready/failed).

## Open Questions

1. **Off-graph property creation UX** — how much structure do we ask for when a
   user vets a property not in our graph (survey no, sub-registrar, address)?
   Minimal fields vs. a guided form.
2. **OCR cost + latency ceiling** — large multi-page deeds through MinerU: what's
   the acceptable per-dossier cost and turnaround, and do we cap pages/size?
3. **Classification eval set** — we need a labeled set of real documents per
   category to measure classifier accuracy. Where does the seed set come from
   (the assignment helps)?
4. **Portal deep-link fragility** — TNREGINET / TN eServices stability and
   whether pre-fill via URL params is even possible, or just deep-link + manual.
5. **Retention / deletion** — how long do we hold confidential user docs, and
   what's the delete-my-dossier guarantee?
6. **Phase-2 boundary** — exactly which verdict (legal opinion vs valuation vs
   reconciliation flags) ships first once the locker has traction.

## Success Criteria

- A user can create a dossier for a property (on- or off-graph), upload ≥1
  document, and within the ingest SLA see it OCR'd and correctly categorized.
- The checklist correctly reports have/missing against the 12-item minimum, and
  the readiness score moves as documents are added.
- For each missing minimum-set doc, the UI shows a correct "go get it" portal
  link.
- A user can ask a question and get an answer grounded in their own uploaded
  documents, with zero cross-user leakage (verified by the ownership predicate;
  a request with another user's `dossier_id` returns nothing).
- A user can **delete a dossier** and the cascade removes: the R2 objects, the
  Neo4j `:Dossier` + `:Document` nodes, the stored OCR text, and (per the
  third-party decision) triggers/records deletion of any MinerU-side copy.
- No user document is ever served from the public bucket or appears in the shared
  graph; confidential docs are never sent to a third party without consent.
- Classification accuracy bar: **sequenced, not set up front.** The assignment
  produces a seed labeled set first; the numeric bar (candidate: ≥85%
  top-category accuracy on the minimum set) is set at eng review *against that
  set*, not before any labeled data exists.

## Distribution Plan

Web service with an existing deployment pipeline (Render backend + Vercel static
frontend). No new distribution channel required. New work rides the current
CI/deploy. The only infra addition is a **private** R2 bucket and its credentials
in the existing secret store.

## Dependencies

- Private R2 bucket + presigned-URL read path (net-new; `pipeline/storage.py` is
  public-only today).
- MinerU OCR in a request context — server-side invocation is already proven
  (`api/review/blocks.py`), so the real dependencies are (a) the third-party data
  handling decision above, (b) the ingest execution model (free-tier host), not
  "can it run server-side."
- A NEW doc-type classifier module for the taxonomy in Appendix A.
  `classify_notice.py` is the binary single/multi notice classifier and is NOT a
  base for this.
- New auth-gated router(s) following the `watchlist` / `conversations` pattern.
- Labeled document eval set (seeded by the assignment) — prerequisite for setting
  the accuracy bar.
- A hosting decision (free-tier sync-with-caps vs paid plan + worker).

## The Assignment

Before any code: **run the locker workflow by hand, once, on ONE real property you
would actually bid on.** Collect every document you can from every source; for
each one, write down where it came from and how long it took to get; and — most
important — record which documents you could **not** get and why. That manual run
gives you three things at once: the real friction map (which sources are slow or
blocked), the seed of a labeled eval set for the classifier, and proof of whether
"what's missing" is genuinely the moment of pain. Bring that list to the eng review.

## Appendix A: Document Taxonomy (classifier labels + checklist source of truth)

The 9 categories and their document types. This is the literal label set for the
doc-type classifier and the source of the full checklist. Each item carries an
`(applicable-if)` note where it is conditional.

**A. Documents from the Bank**
Auction Notice · Sale Notice · Possession Notice · Demand Notice (SARFAESI) ·
Sale Terms & Conditions · Property Inspection Report (if available) · Valuation
Report (if available) · Mortgage Deed / MODT · List of Original Documents held by
the Bank

**B. Title Documents**
Latest Sale Deed · Mother Deed (30–40 yr chain) · All Previous Title Deeds ·
Settlement Deed (if applicable) · Gift Deed (if applicable) · Partition Deed (if
applicable) · Release Deed (if applicable) · Power of Attorney (if applicable) ·
Legal Heir Certificate (if inherited) · Death Certificate (if inherited)

**C. Revenue Records**
Patta · Chitta · A-Register Extract · FMB Sketch (Field Measurement Book) · TSLR
Extract (urban, where applicable)

**D. Encumbrance & Registration Records**
Encumbrance Certificate (EC, preferably 30 yr) · Mortgage Registration Details ·
Mortgage Release Documents (for previous loans, if any)

**E. Layout & Approval Documents**
*Plot:* DTCP / CMDA Approved Layout · Layout Approval Order.
*Flat / Building:* Building Plan Approval · Planning Permission · Completion
Certificate (CC) · Occupancy Certificate (OC) · RERA Registration Details (if
applicable)

**F. Tax & Utility Documents**
Property Tax Receipts · Water Tax Receipts · Electricity Bills · Sewerage Charges
Receipts · Betterment Charges Receipt (if applicable)

**G. Apartment-Specific Documents**
Undivided Share (UDS) Details · Apartment Association No-Due Certificate ·
Maintenance Due Statement · Car Parking Allocation Documents · Builder-Buyer
Agreement (if available)

**H. Legal Verification Documents**
Independent Advocate Legal Opinion · Title Search Report · Court Case Search
Report · Litigation Search Report (if conducted)

**I. Documents Received After Winning Auction**
Bid Acceptance Letter · Sale Confirmation Letter · Sale Certificate · Possession
Letter / Memo · Original Title Documents released by Bank · Handing Over Memo

## Appendix B: The "Absolute Minimum" set (drives the Readiness Score)

12 items the bidder should not skip. In v1 the **10 uploadable** items (1–9, 12)
drive the readiness score (each = 10 points, cap 100). Items 10 & 11 are Phase-2
outputs the locker will help produce; in v1 they are advisory checklist rows that
do not subtract from the score.

1. Sale Deed (B)
2. Mother Deed (B)
3. Encumbrance Certificate (D)
4. Patta (C)
5. FMB Sketch (C)
6. Auction Notice (A)
7. Possession Notice (A)
8. Mortgage Document / MODT (A)
9. Property Tax Receipt (F)
10. Advocate Legal Opinion (H)
11. Court Case Search Report (H)
12. Layout / Building Approval (E)

Letters reference the Appendix A category. Note items 10 and 11 are *outputs the
locker will eventually help produce* (Phase 2 verdicts), so in v1 they count as
"missing → here's how to get one" rather than always-uploadable — flag this
nuance in the checklist copy.
